How to choose a check valve

Flanged check valves (non-return valves) come in C.S (ASTM A216 WCB) and S.S (SS304 or SS316) in CL150 and CL300 from ½" to 12", and C.I in PN16 from 1½" to 8". Wafer disk type, PN10 or PN16, 1½" to 8": C.I or C.S with a D.I or S.S disk, and S.S; an S.S disk wafer check valve is also listed in PN40 (PN25 above DN100) from 1" to 8". Dual-disk wafer: C.I in PN16 and S.S in PN16 or PN25, 2" to 12". The D.I grooved check valve is 300 PSI or PN16, 2½" to 8".

Threaded, ½" to 4": brass swing, brass vertical spring, bronze swing, S.S swing at 200#, S.S vertical spring at 600 or 1000 WOG, and forged S.S piston at 800 WOG with BSP, NPT or SW ends. A forged steel A105 piston check valve, Class 800, NPT or SW, runs from ½" to 2".

Swing types suit horizontal lines with steady flow. Spring and vertical types close faster and work in vertical lines with upward flow. Wafer and dual-disk types are short and fit between flanges, for example after a pump.

Questions buyers ask

Is a check valve the same as a non-return valve?

Yes. Both names mean a valve that lets flow pass one way and stops it coming back.

Do you have a check valve for a vertical line?

Yes. We list a brass vertical spring check valve and an S.S (SS304 or SS316) vertical spring check valve at 600 or 1000 WOG in BSP or NPT, both from ½" to 4".

Which check valve should go after a pump?

Dual-disk and spring types close quickly and are common on pump discharge. Send the line size and flange class on WhatsApp and we will suggest a match from our list.
