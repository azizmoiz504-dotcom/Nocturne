How to choose socket-weld fittings

We supply socket weld fittings, forged in C.S and S.S in CL1000, CL2000, CL3000 and CL6000 from ½" to 4": 45° and 90° elbows, equal and reducing tees, full, half and reducing couplings, unions, equal crosses, end caps and plugs. Reducing tees and couplings run from ⅜" × ¼" to 4" × 3".

Socket weld fittings suit small-bore lines at higher pressure, where a threaded joint could leak and a butt weld is hard to make. Push the pipe into the socket, then pull it back about 1.6 mm before welding to leave an expansion gap. Pick the class to match the pipe: CL3000 is usually paired with SCH80 pipe and CL6000 with SCH160.

For a quote, WhatsApp the fitting, size, class, C.S or S.S, and the quantity.

Questions buyers ask

Class 3000 or 6000?

Match the class to the pipe wall. CL3000 is normally used with SCH80 pipe and CL6000 with SCH160.

Why leave a gap before welding?

The gap of about 1.6 mm lets the pipe expand as the weld cools, which helps stop the weld cracking.

Socket weld or threaded?

Socket weld gives a stronger, leak-free joint. Threaded fittings are easier to take apart. We list both in the same classes.
