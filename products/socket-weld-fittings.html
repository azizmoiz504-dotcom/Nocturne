How to choose socket-weld fittings

Forged C.S socket weld fittings in ASTM A105 are listed to ASME B16.11 in CL3000, CL6000 and CL9000 from ½" to 4": 45° and 90° elbows, equal and reducing tees, full, half and reducing couplings, unions, crosses, end caps, plugs and king nipples. Reducing tees and reducing couplings run from ⅜" × ¼" to 4" × 3".

Socket weld fittings suit small-bore lines at higher pressure, where a threaded joint could leak and a butt weld is hard to make. Push the pipe into the socket, then pull it back about 1.6 mm before welding to leave an expansion gap. Pick the class to match the pipe: CL3000 is usually paired with SCH80 pipe, CL6000 with SCH160 and CL9000 with XXS.

For stainless lines, S.S threaded fittings are on our stainless steel page, and forged C.S threaded fittings are on our threaded forged fittings page.

Questions buyers ask

Class 3000, 6000 or 9000?

Match the class to the pipe wall. CL3000 is normally used with SCH80 pipe, CL6000 with SCH160 and CL9000 with XXS.

Why leave a gap before welding?

The gap of about 1.6 mm lets the pipe expand as the weld cools, which helps stop the weld cracking.

Socket weld or threaded?

Socket weld gives a stronger joint that is less likely to leak. Threaded fittings are easier to take apart; our threaded forged fittings page lists the C.S threaded range.
