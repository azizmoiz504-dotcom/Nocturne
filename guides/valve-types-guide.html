Types of valves and their uses: a short buyer guide

The right valve type for the job saves trouble later. This guide covers the five valve types and the Y-strainer you will meet on most sites, and what each one is for.

Valve types at a glance

Which valve for which job
TypeUse it forAvoid it for

Globe valveThrottling and regulating flowLines where pressure drop must be low

Gate valves

A gate valve lifts a wedge clear of the flow, so when it is fully open the bore is almost unobstructed. Use it for isolation. Left part-open, the wedge vibrates and the seats wear.

Globe valves

A globe valve turns the flow through the body past a plug and seat, which makes it good at regulating flow. The trade-off is a higher pressure drop than a gate or ball valve.

Ball valves

A quarter turn opens or closes a ball valve, so it is fast and easy to see whether it is open. 1PC bodies are compact, 2PC bodies are general purpose, and 3PC bodies can be serviced in the line. A 3-way ball valve diverts flow.

Check valves

A check valve opens with forward flow and closes when flow reverses. Swing types suit horizontal lines; spring and vertical types can work in vertical lines with upward flow; wafer and dual-plate types are short and fit between flanges, such as after a pump.

Butterfly valves

A butterfly valve turns a disc in the bore. It is short and light, which suits large water and cooling lines. Use a lever on smaller sizes and a gear operator on larger ones. A lug body lets you remove the pipe on one side.

Y-strainers

A Y-strainer holds a screen in a side leg to catch scale and debris. Fit it upstream of the item you want to protect, with the leg pointing down on horizontal lines.

Body, class and ends

  • Body: CS (WCB or forged A105) for oil, gas and process lines; SS (CF8, CF8M, 316L) for corrosive or clean service; CI, brass or bronze for water.
  • Class: match the valve to the line, for example CL150, CL300, PN16 or 800 WOG.
  • Ends: flanged for larger lines, NPT, BSPT or SW for small bore. Match the thread to the pipe.

Questions buyers ask

Can I throttle with a ball valve?

Only briefly. Long-term throttling wears the seats. Use a globe valve to regulate flow.

Do I need a Y-strainer before a pump?

It is good practice. A strainer upstream stops scale and weld slag reaching the pump.

Which valve should go after a pump?

Usually a check valve to stop back-flow, plus an isolation valve such as a gate, ball or butterfly valve.
